# Accounting & Finance (Educational)

> General educational reference. Not tax, legal, or investment advice. Consult a licensed CPA / financial advisor for specific situations and jurisdiction rules.

## Double-entry bookkeeping
- Every transaction hits ≥ 2 accounts; total debits = total credits (books always balance).
- **Accounting equation**: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. (Expanded: Equity = Contributed capital + Retained earnings.)
- **Normal balances** — Assets & Expenses increase with **debits**; Liabilities, Equity & Revenue increase with **credits**.
- Mnemonic **DEAD-CLIC**: Debit increases Expenses/Assets/Dividends; Credit increases Liabilities/Income/Capital.

```
Sale of $100 for cash (cost of goods $60):
  Dr Cash            100     Cr Revenue          100
  Dr COGS             60     Cr Inventory         60
Net effect: Assets +40, Equity +40 (via $40 profit). Balances.
```

## The three financial statements
- **Income statement (P&L)** — performance over a *period*. Revenue − COGS = Gross profit; − OpEx = Operating income (EBIT); − interest − tax = Net income. Accrual-based (revenue when earned, expenses when incurred).
- **Balance sheet** — position at a *point in time*. Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Snapshot, not a flow.
- **Cash flow statement** — actual cash movement over a period, in 3 sections: **Operating** (core business), **Investing** (capex, acquisitions, asset sales), **Financing** (debt, equity, dividends).

## How the statements link
- Net income (IS) flows into **retained earnings** (BS equity) and is the starting line of the **indirect cash flow statement**.
- Cash flow statement's ending cash = **Cash** line on the balance sheet.
- Balance-sheet changes drive cash flow: ↑ receivables/inventory = cash *out*; ↑ payables = cash *in*; depreciation added back (non-cash); capex = investing outflow.
- The three tie together every period; if they don't reconcile, something is wrong.

## Accrual vs cash basis
- **Cash basis**: record when cash moves. Simple; used by very small businesses; can distort timing.
- **Accrual basis** (GAAP/IFRS): record when earned/incurred regardless of cash — matching principle pairs revenue with the expenses that produced it.
- Accrual shows economic reality but can show profit while cash-starved -> always read the cash flow statement alongside the P&L.

## Key ratios
- **Gross margin** = gross profit / revenue. **Operating margin** = EBIT / revenue. **Net margin** = net income / revenue.
- **Current ratio** = current assets / current liabilities (≥ 1 = can cover near-term obligations). **Quick ratio** = (current assets − inventory) / current liabilities.
- **ROE** = net income / shareholders' equity. **ROA** = net income / total assets. **ROIC** = NOPAT / invested capital.
- **Debt-to-equity** = total debt / equity (leverage). **Interest coverage** = EBIT / interest expense.
- **Inventory turnover** = COGS / avg inventory; **DSO** = (AR / revenue) x 365; **DPO** = (AP / COGS) x 365.
- Compare ratios to prior periods + industry peers; a ratio alone is meaningless.

## Unit economics + break-even
- **Contribution margin** = price − variable cost per unit (or revenue − variable costs). CM % = CM / price.
- **Break-even units** = fixed costs / contribution margin per unit. **Break-even revenue** = fixed costs / CM %.
- **Gross margin per unit** must be positive before scaling — selling more of a money-losing unit deepens losses.

```
Fixed $10,000/mo; price $50; variable $30 -> CM $20.
Break-even = 10,000 / 20 = 500 units/mo.
```

## Working capital + cash management
- **Working capital** = current assets − current liabilities; funds day-to-day operations.
- **Cash conversion cycle (CCC)** = DSO + DIO − DPO (days from cash-out to cash-back). Shorter = less cash tied up.
- Growth consumes cash: rising receivables + inventory absorb cash even while profitable ("growing broke").
- Levers: invoice fast + collect (↓DSO), lean inventory (↓DIO), negotiate supplier terms (↑DPO), manage runway (cash / monthly burn).
- **Runway** = cash on hand / net monthly burn. **Burn rate** = net monthly cash outflow.

## Budgeting + forecasting
- **Budget** = plan/target for a period; **forecast** = updated expectation given actuals.
- Methods: incremental (last year ± %), **zero-based** (justify every line from zero), driver-based (model outputs from volume/price/headcount drivers), rolling forecast (always N months ahead).
- **Variance analysis** = actual vs budget; investigate material + persistent variances, split price vs volume vs mix.
- Build 3 scenarios (base / upside / downside); stress-test cash runway in the downside.

## Basic tax concepts (general)
- Business is taxed on **taxable income** (revenue − deductible expenses), which differs from book profit (timing + non-deductible items).
- **Depreciation/amortization**: spread the cost of long-lived assets over useful life (non-cash expense).
- Entity type affects taxation (pass-through vs corporate) — see business_law_basics.
- **Deductible** = ordinary + necessary business expenses; keep contemporaneous records/receipts.
- Rates, deductions, credits, deadlines are jurisdiction-specific + change — **consult a CPA/tax professional**; do not rely on this for filing.

## Reading financials (quick checklist)
- P&L: revenue growth, gross margin trend, operating leverage, one-time items stripped out.
- Balance sheet: liquidity (current/quick), leverage (D/E), receivables/inventory buildup, goodwill.
- Cash flow: is operating cash flow positive + tracking net income? Capex vs depreciation? Financing dependence?
- **Free cash flow** = operating cash flow − capex; the cash actually available to investors/reinvestment.
- Read notes + accounting policies; check for revenue-recognition aggressiveness + related-party items.

## Pitfalls -> Fix
- **Confusing profit with cash** -> profitable firms fail from cash gaps; monitor cash flow + runway alongside the P&L.
- **Ignoring working capital in growth plans** -> model the cash absorbed by receivables + inventory before scaling; secure funding early.
- **Reading only the income statement** -> always triangulate all three statements; net income can be inflated while cash bleeds.
- **Vanity revenue, ignoring margin** -> track gross + contribution margin; unprofitable unit economics don't fix with scale.
- **Mixing personal + business finances** -> separate accounts + clean books; commingling breaks reporting + liability protection.
- **Cash-basis blindness on obligations** -> accrue liabilities (taxes, payroll, deferred revenue) so you don't spend money you owe.
- **No budget-to-actual review** -> monthly close + variance analysis; catch drift while it's small.
- **Treating depreciation/deferred revenue as cash** -> non-cash + liability items aren't spendable; reconcile to the cash statement.
- **Over-leveraging** -> keep interest coverage healthy + covenants monitored; debt amplifies both returns and ruin.
- **DIY complex tax/accounting** -> engage a CPA for tax filing, revenue recognition, entity/structure, and audits.

## Cost concepts
- **Fixed** (rent, salaries) vs **variable** (materials, per-unit) vs **semi-variable**; behavior with volume drives break-even + margin.
- **Direct** (traceable to a product) vs **indirect/overhead** (shared, must be allocated).
- **Sunk cost** (already spent, irrelevant to decisions) vs **opportunity cost** (next-best forgone) vs **marginal cost** (cost of one more unit) — decisions use marginal + opportunity, never sunk.
- **Gross vs net**: gross = before deductions/costs; net = after. Always confirm which a figure refers to.

## Financing + time value
- **Time value of money**: a dollar today > a dollar later; discount future cash flows to **present value** (PV = FV / (1+r)^n).
- **NPV** (net present value): sum of discounted cash flows − initial investment; NPV > 0 = value-creating. **IRR** = discount rate where NPV = 0.
- **Debt vs equity**: debt is cheaper (tax-deductible interest) but adds fixed obligations + risk; equity dilutes ownership but no repayment.
- **Payback period** = time to recover an investment (simple, ignores time value — pair with NPV).

## Financial controls
- Segregation of duties (no one person controls a full transaction cycle), approvals + authorization limits, reconciliations (bank, AR, AP), and an audit trail deter error + fraud.
- Monthly **close** process + review; timely, accurate books enable decisions — stale books = flying blind.

Standards (GAAP vs IFRS), tax law, and thresholds vary by jurisdiction + entity — verify specifics with a qualified professional.
