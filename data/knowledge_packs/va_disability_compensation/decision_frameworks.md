# Decision Frameworks — VA Rating Reasoning

## Which percentage applies?
- Map the documented symptoms/severity/measurements to the DC's levels; pick the level
  the picture **more nearly approximates** (§4.7). Tie / reasonable doubt → claimant (§4.3).
- Ratings reflect **average impairment of earning capacity** (§4.1), not isolated symptoms.

## Which diagnostic code?
- Listed condition → its DC. Unlisted → **analogous** DC (§4.20) with the closest
  anatomy/symptomatology. Don't rate the same impairment twice (§4.14 pyramiding).

## Single vs. multiple ratings
- Separate, distinct disabilities → separate ratings, then **combine (§4.25)**.
- Overlapping symptoms of one disability → one rating (avoid pyramiding).
- Paired extremities → apply the **bilateral factor (§4.26)**.

## Schedular vs. TDIU vs. extra-schedular
- If the schedule captures the impairment → schedular rating.
- If service-connected disabilities prevent gainful work → **TDIU (§4.16)** at the 100%
  rate (typical thresholds: one ≥60%, or combined ≥70% with one ≥40%).
- If the schedule doesn't adequately reflect an exceptional disability picture →
  extra-schedular referral (§3.321).

## Trade-offs / cautions
- Higher individual ratings don't add linearly — §4.25 combination yields less than the sum.
- A 0% rating still has value (preserves service connection for future worsening).
- The schedule is amended over time; a criterion may differ from older memory — verify
  against current eCFR.
