# Sales & CRM

## Sales process (stage -> objective)
- **Prospect** -> build a targeted list of accounts/contacts fitting the ICP (ideal customer profile).
- **Qualify** -> confirm need, budget, authority, timing; disqualify fast to protect time.
- **Present / demo** -> map product to the buyer's specific pain + desired outcome, not a feature dump.
- **Handle objections** -> surface + resolve concerns (price, timing, trust, competitor, status quo).
- **Close** -> ask for the commitment, agree terms, get signature/PO.
- **Follow-up / expand** -> onboard, ensure value, then upsell/cross-sell/renew and ask for referrals.
- Advance on *buyer* actions, not seller optimism; every stage needs a defined exit criterion.

## Qualification frameworks
- **BANT**: Budget, Authority, Need, Timing — fast, classic, transactional.
- **MEDDIC / MEDDPICC**: Metrics (quantified value), Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, Champion (+ Paper process, Competition) — for complex/enterprise deals.
- **SPIN** selling questions: Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff — uncover + amplify pain before pitching.
- **CHAMP**: Challenges, Authority, Money, Prioritization — pain-first variant of BANT.
- Rule: a deal with no identified pain + no economic buyer + no champion is not qualified, regardless of interest.

## Pipeline, stages, forecasting
- **Pipeline** = all open opportunities by stage; each stage has a probability weighting.
- **Weighted pipeline** = Σ (deal value x stage probability). Use for coverage, not commit forecasting.
- **Pipeline coverage ratio** = open pipeline / quota; target ~3-4x to hit quota given typical win rates.
- **Forecast categories**: Commit (high confidence) > Best case > Pipeline > Omitted. Forecast on categories + close dates, not raw weighted math.
- **Win rate** = won / (won + lost). **Sales velocity** = (# opps x avg deal size x win rate) / avg sales cycle length — four levers to grow revenue.
- Keep close dates + amounts honest; sandbagging and happy ears both break forecasts.

## CRM usage
- Core objects: **Leads/Contacts** (people), **Accounts** (companies), **Deals/Opportunities** (revenue events), **Activities** (calls, emails, meetings, tasks), **Products/line items**.
- Log every meaningful touch; the CRM is the single source of truth — "if it's not in the CRM, it didn't happen."
- Standardize stage definitions + required fields per stage (enter/exit criteria) so data is comparable.
- Automate: activity capture, task reminders, sequences/cadences, stage-based alerts, reporting dashboards.
- Hygiene = deduped records, next-step + close date on every open deal, aged deals reviewed/closed.

## Lead scoring
- **Fit** (firmographic/demographic: right industry, size, role) + **Intent/engagement** (behavior: site visits, demo requests, email clicks, content downloads).
- Score numerically; route MQL (marketing-qualified) -> SQL (sales-accepted) at a threshold.
- Negative scoring for disqualifiers (student email, competitor, wrong geo).
- Recency matters: intent decays — speed-to-lead (respond in minutes) sharply raises contact + conversion rates.

## Inbound vs outbound
- **Inbound**: prospect comes to you (content, SEO, referrals, demo requests); warmer, lower CAC, scales with content, less predictable volume.
- **Outbound**: you initiate (cold email, calls, LinkedIn, ABM); predictable, targetable, higher effort/CAC, needs strong list + messaging.
- **ABM** (account-based marketing): sales + marketing target a named account list with coordinated multi-threaded outreach — for high-ACV enterprise.
- Most orgs run both; balance by ACV, sales-cycle length, and TAM.

## SaaS / recurring-revenue metrics
- **MRR / ARR**: monthly / annual recurring revenue. ARR = MRR x 12.
- **New / Expansion / Contraction / Churned MRR** — decompose MRR movement each month.
- **Logo churn** = customers lost / starting customers. **Revenue churn** = $ lost / starting $ (net or gross).
- **NRR / NDR** (net revenue retention) = (start + expansion − contraction − churn) / start. > 100% = growth from existing base alone; > 110% is strong.
- **Quota**: annual sales target per rep; **attainment** = actual / quota; **ramp** = time to full productivity.
- **ACV** = annual contract value; **TCV** = total contract value incl. multi-year + one-time.

## Objection handling + negotiation
- Framework: **acknowledge -> clarify (question) -> respond with evidence -> confirm resolved**. Don't argue.
- Common objections: price ("too expensive"), timing ("not now"), authority ("need to check"), trust ("does it work?"), status quo ("we're fine").
- Price objection: reframe to value/ROI + cost of inaction; isolate ("if price weren't an issue, would you move forward?").
- **Negotiation**: know your BATNA + walkaway; anchor first when informed; trade concessions, never give unilaterally ("if you can X, I can Y"); protect price by adding/removing scope, not just discounting; get budget before quoting.
- Silence after asking for the close is leverage — don't talk first.

## Sales-marketing alignment (SLA)
- Shared definitions: ICP, MQL, SQL, and lead handoff criteria + SLA (response time, follow-up count).
- Closed-loop feedback: sales reports lead quality back to marketing; marketing reports campaign source on won deals.
- Shared pipeline + revenue goal ("smarketing"); joint QBRs on funnel conversion by stage.

## Pitfalls -> Fix
- **Happy ears** (hearing buying signals that aren't there) -> require explicit qualification + next-step commitment; disqualify on missing pain/authority/budget.
- **No follow-up** (most deals need 5+ touches) -> build cadences/sequences; task every open deal with a scheduled next step.
- **Reflexive discounting** -> lead with value/ROI; trade concessions for scope/term/referral; get manager approval on discounts.
- **Poor pipeline hygiene** (stale deals, no close date/next step) -> weekly review; auto-flag aged/no-activity deals; close-lost dead deals.
- **Pitching before diagnosing** -> SPIN/discovery first; demo only what maps to identified pain.
- **Single-threaded deals** (one contact) -> multi-thread to economic buyer + champion + users; deals die when your one contact leaves.
- **Sandbagging / commit-inflation** -> tie forecast categories to objective exit criteria, not gut feel.
- **Chasing every lead equally** -> lead-score + prioritize by fit x intent x deal size; speed-to-lead on hot ones.
- **Ignoring existing customers** -> NRR > 100% is cheaper growth than new logos; build expansion + renewal motions.
- **CRM as after-the-fact data entry** -> make it the workflow (sequences, tasks, dashboards) so reps use it to sell, not just report.
- **No loss analysis** -> log lost reasons; review win/loss to fix ICP, messaging, and pricing.

## Discovery + demo
- **Discovery** is the highest-leverage stage: quantify pain (cost of the problem), map decision process + criteria, identify economic buyer + champion, confirm timeline + budget reality.
- Ask open questions; talk < 40% of the time; summarize back to confirm understanding ("so if I heard right…").
- **Demo**: tailor to the pains uncovered; lead with the "aha" outcome, not a feature tour; tie each feature to a stated need; end with a clear next step + mutual action plan.

## Sales activity + productivity
- **Leading indicators** (activities: calls, emails, meetings booked, opps created) predict **lagging** results (revenue, quota attainment) — manage the leading ones.
- **Mutual action plan** (MAP): jointly agreed steps + dates + owners to close; keeps complex deals on track + surfaces stalls early.
- Prioritize by deal value x win probability x time-to-close; don't let small easy deals crowd out big strategic ones (or vice versa).
- **Referrals** convert highest + cost least — ask satisfied customers systematically at value milestones.

## Compensation + motion
- Comp plan drives behavior: base + variable (commission/bonus); align accelerators to the metric you actually want (new logos vs expansion vs margin).
- **Sales motions** by ACV: self-serve/PLG (low touch, high volume), transactional/inside sales (mid), field/enterprise (high touch, multi-threaded, long cycle).
- Wrong motion for the price point wastes money — don't put field reps on a $500 product or self-serve on a $500K deal.

Adapt frameworks to deal size + cycle; enterprise needs MEDDIC + multi-threading, SMB/transactional needs speed + volume.
