# Stakeholder Management
## Identify + register
- Stakeholder = anyone who affects or is affected by the project (internal + external): sponsor, users, customers, team, PMO, regulators, vendors, ops/support.
- Stakeholder register: id, name/role, interest, influence, expectations, engagement level, comms preference, notes. Living document.
- Miss no one: silent-but-powerful stakeholders (legal, security, finance, regulators) sink projects late.
## Power/Interest grid (Mendelow)
- Axes: Power (high/low) × Interest (high/low). Strategy per quadrant:
  - High power + high interest = **Manage closely** (key players; sponsor).
  - High power + low interest = **Keep satisfied** (don't bore, don't blindside).
  - Low power + high interest = **Keep informed** (allies, detail-hungry).
  - Low power + low interest = **Monitor** (minimal effort).
- Variants: Power/Influence, Influence/Impact, Salience model (power + legitimacy + urgency).
## Engagement levels
- Scale: Unaware -> Resistant -> Neutral -> Supportive -> Leading.
- Assess Current (C) vs Desired (D) per stakeholder in an engagement assessment matrix; the gap drives engagement actions.
- Example: `Sponsor: C=Neutral, D=Leading -> gap; action: weekly 1:1 + tie project to their OKR`.
## RACI matrix
- Responsible (does the work), Accountable (one only, owns the outcome / sign-off), Consulted (two-way input), Informed (one-way updates).
- Rules: exactly one A per row/activity; R and A can be the same person; too many C's = slow; a row of all I = orphaned.
- Example row: `Deploy release | Dev: R | Eng Mgr: A | Security: C | Support: I`.
- Variants: RASCI (S = Support), RACI-VS (Verify/Sign-off), DACI (Driver/Approver) for decisions.
## Communication plan
- Columns: audience, message/what, purpose, frequency/when, channel, sender, format.
- Match channel to need: decisions/conflict = synchronous/verbal; status/record = written/async; executives = dashboard + exception reporting.
- Comms channels grow as n(n-1)/2 — 10 people = 45 channels; a plan controls the explosion.
- Push (email, reports), Pull (wiki, dashboard), Interactive (meetings, calls) — pick per message.
## Managing expectations + influence
- Set expectations early and in writing; surface bad news fast and yourself. Under-promise, over-deliver beats the reverse.
- Influence without authority: reciprocity, consistency/commitment, social proof, credibility, framing to their interests, building coalitions, trading value (Cohen-Bradford currencies).
- Manage up: give the sponsor decisions to make, not problems to solve; bring options + a recommendation.
## Escalation
- Escalate when a blocker exceeds your authority, crosses org boundaries, or a decision stalls past a deadline.
- Escalate the issue, not the person; bring context + options + the ask + a needed-by date; follow the agreed path.
## Difficult stakeholders
- Silent resistor: engage 1:1, find the underlying concern. Over-committer/scope-adder: route to change control. Absent sponsor: escalate or re-confirm mandate. Conflicting stakeholders: surface trade-offs to a single decision authority.
## Salience & prioritization
- Salience model: attributes = Power + Legitimacy + Urgency; combinations rank stakeholders (definitive = all three).
- Prioritize engagement effort by salience/grid position; don't spend equal effort on everyone.
## Meetings & cadence
- Match cadence to the audience: exec steering monthly + exception; team daily; sponsor 1:1 weekly; broad update biweekly.
- Every meeting: purpose, agenda, right attendees, decisions + actions with owners captured and circulated.
- Exception/by-exception reporting to executives: surface only variances needing a decision, not raw status.
## Conflict & negotiation
- Thomas-Kilmann modes: competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, accommodating — pick by stakes + relationship.
- Interest-based negotiation (separate people from problem, focus on interests not positions, invent options, objective criteria).
- Build a coalition of supporters before a contested decision; neutralize resistors by addressing the real concern.
## Building trust
- Deliver on small commitments to bank credibility; transparency + consistency compound trust.
- Reciprocity: give help/information first; social capital cashes in when you need influence later.
## Communication mechanics
- 55/38/7 (Mehrabian) applies to feelings/attitude: for emotionally charged messages, prefer face-to-face over text.
- Feedback loops: confirm receipt + understanding, don't assume the message landed; close the loop on decisions.
- Sender-receiver model: encode -> transmit (with noise) -> decode; reduce noise with clear, jargon-free, audience-fit messages.
## RACI build steps
- List activities (rows) × roles (columns); assign R/A/C/I per cell; validate each row has exactly one A and at least one R.
- Review with stakeholders; resolve gaps (no R), overloads (too many A/C), and orphans (all I).
## Expectation baselines
- Document agreed scope, success criteria, and reporting cadence up front; refer back to them when scope pressure arrives.
- Manage the "expectation gap": perceived value = delivery minus expectation; control both sides.
## Pitfalls -> Fix
- **Identifying only obvious stakeholders**: security/legal/regulators appear late and block. Fix: systematic identification; scan org chart + external parties; re-scan each phase.
- **Register written once**: influence/interest shift over time. Fix: revisit the register + power/interest grid at each phase gate.
- **One-size-fits-all communication**: execs get flooded, users get starved. Fix: tailor per quadrant (manage closely / keep satisfied / keep informed / monitor).
- **RACI with two Accountables (or none)**: nobody owns the outcome. Fix: exactly one A per activity.
- **RACI overloaded with Consulted**: decisions crawl. Fix: minimize C; distinguish genuine input from FYI (Informed).
- **Managing stakeholders only at kickoff and go-live**: engagement decays, surprises pile up. Fix: continuous engagement per the comms plan cadence.
- **Hiding bad news / hoping it resolves**: destroys trust when it surfaces. Fix: escalate early with options; be the first to raise the problem.
- **Confusing engagement (attitude) with communication (info flow)**: informed but still resistant. Fix: track C-vs-D engagement gaps and act on attitude, not just message delivery.
- **Treating the loudest stakeholder as the most important**: volume != power/interest. Fix: use the grid; weight by actual power + legitimacy.
- **No single decision authority for conflicts**: endless debate, thrash. Fix: name the accountable decider; surface trade-offs to them.
- **Escalating the person, not the issue**: burns relationships. Fix: escalate facts + options + ask; keep it about the problem.
- **Assuming silence = agreement**: passive resistors derail quietly. Fix: solicit explicit commitment; probe non-participation 1:1.
